# Verified Facts: Migrant Children, Learning Continuity and FLN (India)

Fact-check for the offline QR "learning passport" pitch deck.
Checked: 24 Nov 2026. Every row below comes from a page or PDF that was opened during this check. PDFs were downloaded and their text extracted, and the quotes were copied from that text.
Rows marked "(tool extract)" are quotes taken through a web-reader tool that returns short verbatim fragments. They are probably exact, but check them in the source before printing them on a slide.

Confidence key: **High** = primary/official source, exact text checked. **Medium** = reputable secondary source, or a primary source read only in fragments. **Low** = needs re-checking.

**What the sources do NOT say (based on what I read):** None of the three APAAR sources (brochure, PIB July 2026, portal) mentions offline use, a QR or offline credential, FLN skill levels, or guidance on what a teacher should teach next. The documents describe it as an ID plus a digital record and credential system (DigiLocker, ABC, UDISE+). Access is described as digital ("Accessible through DigiLocker & ABC platform"). I did not find an explicit statement either way on whether it works without internet, so do not claim "APAAR needs internet" as a sourced fact. A safe framing: "APAAR is designed for records and credentials, stored digitally. It is not a teaching tool for FLN levels."

## Recommended 3 strongest stats for the Problem slide

1. **About 80% of temporary migrant children in seven Indian cities lacked access to education near their worksites, and 40% worked.** UNESCO GEM Report 2019. https://gem-report-2019.unesco.org/chapter/introduction/internal-migration
2. **10 million children (2013) lived in rural households with a seasonal-worker family member.** UNESCO GEM Report team, World Education Blog, 2018. https://world-education-blog.org/2018/11/30/india-has-some-of-the-largest-internal-migration-movements-in-the-world-how-has-its-education-adapted/ (say "10 million", not "10.7 million", unless you verify the 10.7 figure in the report itself).
3. **Only 33.7% of Std III children in rural India can do a 2-digit subtraction, and only 23.4% (government schools) can read a Std II text.** ASER 2024. https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER-2024-National-findings.pdf

Optional 4th stat on language: Census 2011 recorded **19,569 raw mother-tongue returns and 121 languages**, while NEP 2020 says teaching should be in the home language until at least Grade 5.

Prepare for judges: Maharashtra's **Digital Education Guarantee Card** (Govt of Maharashtra and Tata Trusts, pilot 2015) already carried migrant children's records and "subject competencies". CPR/UNICEF (2021) found children still could not continue learning because there were no schools near worksites. Present it as prior art and explain what is different about your product (offline QR, FLN levels, shown in the teacher's language).
